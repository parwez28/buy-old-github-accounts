# 20 Steps to Buy Old Github Accounts (Badges & Secure)

> A complete walkthrough for buying an aged GitHub account the right way —
> from deciding what you need, to picking the right badges, to locking down
> the transfer securely. Based on how [allgit.dev](https://allgit.dev) works.

---

## Steps 1–4: Know what you're buying

### Step 1 — Define why you need an old account

Before browsing anything, write down your actual goal. Freelancers need
client-facing credibility. Job seekers need résumé-matching history.
Agencies need presentable team profiles. Bot operators just need a working
login with fewer rate limits. Your goal determines everything that follows —
tier, budget, and which badges matter.

### Step 2 — Decide who will look at the profile

If humans (clients, recruiters, maintainers) will inspect the profile,
history depth and badges matter enormously. If only machines will use it
(CI runners, scrapers, automation), age still helps with limits, but badges
are irrelevant. Don't pay for prestige nobody will see.

### Step 3 — Learn how GitHub badges work

Badges are GitHub's official achievements shown on a profile. Earned badges
(Pull Shark, Starstruck, Galaxy Brain, Pair Extraordinaire) reflect real
activity. Time-locked badges (Arctic Code Vault, Mars 2020) prove the
account existed during historic windows — and can never be earned again.
Understanding this split is the key to judging any listing.

### Step 4 — Understand why old beats new

A new account is a blank page: zero contributions, zero trust signals, the
strictest rate limits, and the most aggressive spam filtering. An old
account with real history compresses years of credibility-building into day
one — and carries badges that new accounts can literally never obtain.

## Steps 5–8: Pick the right account

### Step 5 — Choose your tier

- **Fresh (0–6 months):** cheapest, clean slate — good for bots and
secondary accounts.
- **Aged (1–2 years):** the sweet spot — real history and early badges at a
moderate price.
- **Veteran (3–4 years):** deep history plus rare badges like Arctic Code
Vault — for maximum authority.
- **Legend (5+ years):** the rarest — frozen-in-time badges and often an
existing follower base.

### Step 6 — Set your budget

Prices are listed in USD. Decide your ceiling before you browse: the tier
jump from Aged to Veteran is significant, and Legend inventory is priced as
the collectible it is. Knowing your budget keeps you from falling in love
with a profile you shouldn't buy.

### Step 7 — Browse the inventory

Go to [allgit.dev](https://allgit.dev) and look through what's actually in
stock. Every listing documents the account's history, repositories, stars,
followers, and badges. Treat it like shopping for anything else: compare
multiple options before committing.

### Step 8 — Shortlist 2–3 candidates

Never buy the first profile you see. Shortlist a few across your budget
range and compare them side by side: age vs. price, badge portfolios,
follower counts, and how natural the contribution history looks.

## Steps 9–12: Verify before you pay

### Step 9 — Inspect the contribution graph

Real history has texture: busy weeks and quiet weeks, varied commit times,
work across multiple repositories. A suspiciously uniform graph is a red
flag. AllGit.dev hand-verifies accounts for natural patterns — but train
your own eye anyway.

### Step 10 — Verify the badges on the listing

Cross-check every claimed badge against what you learned in Step 3. If a
listing claims Arctic Code Vault, the account must predate mid-2020. If the
ages don't line up, walk away. Badge claims are the easiest thing to verify
— and the easiest place for a bad seller to lie.

### Step 11 — Ask about custom requirements

Need a specific creation year, a minimum follower count, or an exact badge
combo? Message the platform before buying. Custom sourcing is available —
if it's in inventory, you can get it. It's far better to ask first than to
settle for a close-enough profile.

### Step 12 — Confirm what's included

Make sure you know exactly what the purchase covers: the GitHub credentials,
the attached email account, and the transfer checklist. No surprises after
payment.

## Steps 13–16: Buy and take delivery

### Step 13 — Choose your payment method

Checkout is in cryptocurrency — Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, and other major
coins — through an encrypted flow. Pick whichever coin you hold; the USD
price converts automatically at checkout.

### Step 14 — Complete the secure checkout

Payments confirm automatically on-chain, so there's no manual approval wait
and no card details involved. Double-check the wallet address and amount
before sending — crypto transactions don't reverse.

### Step 15 — Receive your credentials

Within minutes of confirmation, login details arrive by email and Telegram.
Premium tiers get a final manual quality check first, but delivery is still
same-day, guaranteed. Save these credentials somewhere safe immediately.

### Step 16 — Log in and look around

Sign in, confirm the profile matches the listing — history, badges,
followers, repositories. If anything doesn't match what you paid for,
contact support right away under the replacement warranty.

---

## Steps 17–20: Lock it down

### Step 17 — Change the password immediately

Your first action as owner: set a strong, unique password that only you
know. Use a password manager. This single step severs the most obvious
remaining link to the handover.

### Step 18 — Replace the email and enable 2FA

Add your personal email as the primary address, then enable two-factor
authentication. Generate fresh recovery codes and store them offline. From
this point, the account's recovery paths point only to you.

### Step 19 — Audit sessions, keys, and apps

Review active sessions and revoke anything unfamiliar. Remove old SSH keys
and deploy keys. Check authorized OAuth apps and disconnect anything you
didn't add. This is the same hardening any security engineer runs on a new
account — don't skip it.

### Step 20 — Use it — and know your warranty

You're done. The account is exclusively yours. If any access issue appears
within the warranty period, the replacement warranty covers a free swap for
an equivalent or better profile — and support runs 24/7 via Telegram and
WhatsApp.
